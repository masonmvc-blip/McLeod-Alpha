## Executive Assessment

May 14 supplies a controlled side-by-side comparison. Two fresh, structurally
bounded scalps completed cleanly: May 16 587 puts from `3.34` to `3.60` after a
downside OMG close, bounce, doji, and renewed decline; and May 16 587 calls from
`3.79` to `3.94` on a support-to-50-MA recovery. Both exits occurred at nearby
structure. The call exit proved especially instructive because price reversed
immediately after briefly reaching the target.

At the same time, the prior day's 582 puts sat near `1.85` versus a `3.11`
entry, about 40% down. The source considered exiting, then deferred the decision
to the next day's economic news. A new May 23 589-call scalp at `5.94` also
remained open after its `6.10` target failed, and a late May 23 587-call trade
at `6.50` was unresolved at the recording end.

The strongest lesson is that the quality difference came from exit definition,
not directional accuracy. The completed trades named the nearby destination
before or immediately after entry and accepted it. The unresolved positions
depended on time, future news, or confidence that the market would return.

## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. **The best scalps had a destination, not a prediction.** The put targeted
   gap support; the call targeted the 50-MA/resistance pocket.
2. **Place the exit immediately.** The source nearly missed the call target
   while narrating. A pre-staged limit reduces attention-dependent failure.
3. **Do not raise a scalp target because breakout seems possible.** The `3.94`
   exit was followed almost immediately by reversal.
4. **Let support change the trade.** The source declined a second downside
   entry and flipped only after support produced a double bottom and recovery.
5. **Overnight news is not a repair mechanism.** Holding a 40%-down put for a
   future release adds gap and theta risk without restoring the original setup.
6. **Opposing positions need aggregate-risk accounting.** Long calls and puts
   can both lose through decay, spread, or poorly timed exits.

## Contradictions and Process Risks

- The source said it did not want the old puts to erase recent gains, yet still
  lacked a deterministic exit after they fell about 40%.
- The 589-call trade was called a scalp, but remaining expiration and broader
  confidence were used to justify holding after the target failed.
- The speakers correctly required confirmation during later congestion, but
  the early 589 calls were entered before key resistance cleared.
- New winning scalps may obscure the unresolved loss; session-level gross wins
  do not reconcile total portfolio P&L.
- Several positions were open in opposing directions without disclosed size,
  delta, theta, or combined maximum loss.

## Falsifiable Replay Hypotheses

1. Compare support-to-first-resistance scalp exits with holding for acceptance
   through resistance.
2. Test immediate target placement versus manual target entry under narration
   or multitasking latency.
3. Compare downside OMG close-bounce-renewed-weakness entries with immediate
   post-close entries near gap support.
4. Test a fixed carry-loss invalidation against holding through scheduled news
   after a same-session scalp premise fails.
5. Measure combined P&L and decay for opposing open calls and puts versus
   closing the invalidated leg before adding a new position.
