# McLeod Alpha Research Report: 2025-06-16 Trading Room

## Executive Assessment

June 16 rewarded upside acceptance and continuation. After an initial close
above the original OMG boundary and a one-minute pullback, 20 June 20 602 calls
entered at `3.41` and later sold at `3.76`. June 20 601 OMG calls entered at
`3.99`, had a `4.23` target, and filled at `4.38` during the next surge. The
model-call net was narrated as both `$680` and `$690`, so the premium path is
clear but the exact net is not.

A later gap-fill continuation was less clean. June 20 603 calls entered at
`3.60` with a target reduced from `3.95` to `3.92`. The option traded into the
mid-`3.80s`, then reversed with stronger downside volume. The presenter
acknowledged that the trade should have been closed earlier and reported a
`3.72` simulator exit; another execution was reported at `3.78`. Taking the
smaller gain when the gap-fill attempt failed was better than preserving the
original target.

The strongest negative lesson came afterward. Despite a persistent uptrend,
the room advised people holding puts to stay in because markets eventually
reverse and because later expiration provides time. That is not an
invalidation rule. In contrast, the presenter later declined a tempting put
setup because of fatigue and missing one-minute confirmation. The reusable
lesson is to preserve the latter discipline: accepted breakouts may be traded,
failed continuations may be exited, and adverse positions should not be held
solely because time remains.

## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. The strongest admission combined a boundary close, retest, and renewed
   buying pressure.
2. When a gap-fill continuation rejects its target and downside volume
   strengthens, preserve the available gain.
3. Reconcile premium arithmetic separately from claimed net profit.
4. Remaining time to expiration is not an invalidation or a reason to retain a
   losing intraday thesis.
5. Fatigue and missing confirmation are valid reasons not to trade.
