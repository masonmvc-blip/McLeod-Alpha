## Executive Assessment

This recording contrasts one clean, bounded call scalp with several overlapping
and unresolved positions. After SPY recovered above resistance, May 30 583
calls entered at `7.07` and were sold at `7.42` when price met minor
resistance. The original `7.56` target reportedly traded later, but the
35-cent exit matched the structure visible in the source.

The downside OMG was weaker. May 30 582 puts entered at `6.48` with a `6.87`
target tied to SPY near `580.80`. SPY instead reversed upward. The speaker
considered cutting the loss, then retained the puts on an unconfirmed
head-and-shoulders possibility and later treated a return to entry as a
"get-out-of-jail" outcome. The position remained unresolved at recording end.

The strongest reusable lesson is that a thesis can remain plausible while the
trade has become poor. Once price reclaimed resistance and held support, the
put needed a predefined invalidation or time stop; additional chart narratives
and the attraction of breakeven were not substitutes.

## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. A confirmed breakdown still needs room beyond the first meaningful support.
2. When price reclaims the failed-break level, invalidate the short thesis
   rather than inventing a new pattern while already positioned.
3. Taking gain at observed resistance is defensible even if a later target
   trades.
4. Breakeven is an account outcome, not a market-structure signal.
5. Track each presenter and account separately before evaluating aggregate
   exposure.

- The put holder repeatedly identified support and bullish recovery but did
  not report a stop.
- The rationale for holding shifted among chart patterns, macro risk, holiday

## Falsifiable Replay Hypotheses

1. Exit downside OMG trades when SPY closes back above the broken boundary and
   holds it for two one-minute bars.
2. Reject targets requiring price to cross a previously identified
   retracement/support cluster.
3. Compare first-resistance exits with fixed premium targets after opening
   reversals.
4. Measure whether simultaneous opposing positions reduce loss or merely add
   spread, complexity, and delayed exits.
