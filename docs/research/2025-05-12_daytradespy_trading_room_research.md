# McLeod Alpha Research Report: 2025-05-12 Trading Room

## Executive Assessment

The complete authorized transcript captures a gap-and-profit-taking session
after the U.S.-China tariff truce. The best source-reported trades did not
depend on predicting the whole day: a May 16 downside OMG moved from `4.41` to
`4.72`, and two May 23 put scalps moved from `6.39` to `6.60` and `6.46` to
`6.60`. A much larger zero-day 580-put position moved from `2.50` to `2.73`.
The source explicitly called that trade aggressive and later explained that a
bounce could destroy a zero-day position.

The most reusable lesson is narrower than the day's large reported dollars:
when expiration risk is extreme, take the available gain at nearby structure.
The 230-contract zero-day position originally sought `3.00`, was moved to
`3.15`, then repeatedly revised while SPY found support near `578.50`. It was
finally closed at `2.73`. That 23-cent exit avoided the later recovery that the
source itself said could be fatal to the contract.

The source also supplied the counterexample. A May 16 579-call scalp entered at
`5.14` before the double-bottom recovery was fully accepted, then became a
rest-of-week hold when price moved against it. A second 580-call position at
`5.05` remained open at recording end after its small target failed. The
session therefore reinforces the corpus-wide rule: a scalp should not acquire
more time and a broader thesis merely because its immediate premise failed.

## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. **Zero-day risk demands a nearby, executable exit.** The source reduced the
   580-put target as support held and escaped before the rebound.
2. **Place the option target before underlying support.** The OMG target was
   linked to SPY `578.60`, just above the visible support pocket.
3. **Choose expiration to match attention.** The presenter used May 23 puts for
   a narrated live trade because the extra time made management less brittle.
4. **Do not convert a failed scalp into a weekly thesis.** Both call positions
   outlived their immediate targets without a reported stop.
5. **Treat a large prior win as a risk state.** The source explicitly recognized
   giddiness and advised stopping; that rule should be measurable, not merely
   conversational.
6. **Do not count unsubmitted or retrospective trades.** The transcript clearly
   separates queued ideas, signal calculations, and reported fills.

## Contradictions and Process Risks

- The 230-contract trade was called "going for broke" while the same session
  emphasized survival and avoiding losses.
- The call entered at `5.14` was called premature, yet adverse movement was
  answered by extending the horizon rather than enforcing an invalidation.
- The 580-call trade targeted only `0.15`, then was retained after breaking a
  stated underlying concern near `578.35`.
- Gross-dollar claims omit account equity, fees, spread, slippage, and whether
  all 230 contracts could exit at the stated price.
- Weekend call testimonials may create survivorship and outcome bias; losing
  rule-following exits were discussed but not displayed with comparable
  emotional weight.

## Falsifiable Replay Hypotheses

1. Compare zero-day exits taken at the first nearby support with holding for the
   original larger premium target, using executable bid/ask paths.
2. Compare downside OMG close entries with first-touch entries on large gap
   days, segmented by room to the nearest support.
3. Test mandatory closure when a scalp target fails and the underlying breaks
   its admission structure versus extending expiration-based recovery holds.
4. Measure whether a large prior-session or overnight gain predicts worse
   subsequent entry discipline unless trading stops for the day.
5. Compare short-dated and next-week contracts for the same setup after
   controlling for delta, spread, and management latency.
