## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. **Match the target to available room.** The completed put scalps sought only
   the nearby range boundary or support pocket. Small, structurally available
   moves produced clear exits.
2. **Do not let a scalp silently become a recovery position.** The 567-call
   premise targeted only `0.13`, yet the position remained open after falling
   approximately `0.58` and later much more. A failed scalp needs an immediate
   invalidation defined before entry.
3. **A chart touch is not an option fill.** The source explicitly recognized
   that the early aggressive target moved too quickly to assume execution. This
   is an important constraint for replay.
4. **Close, bounce, and renewed weakness improved the OMG entry.** The formal
   downside trade waited for the close and a bounce before entry. The source
   also caught a wrong contract selection before submitting.
5. **Support and time justified taking the attainable gain.** Near the OMG
   target, the source considered the approaching session end and nearby support
   rather than treating the exact target as sacred.
6. **Losing positions impose an attention cost.** While managing and defending
   the calls, the source twice acknowledged missing cleaner downside trades.
7. **Weekend risk requires a rule before Friday entry.** The late plan to take
   half off near SPY `563` was better than no limit, but it appeared only after
   the position was deeply adverse.

## Contradictions and Process Risks

- The source described the 567-call entry as a scalp, then justified continued
  holding with weekly expiry, broad accumulation, a 60-minute bullish view, and
  potential weekend recovery.
- The source acknowledged severe near-term bearish structure while retaining
  calls because of the larger bullish thesis.
- An explicit stop emerged only late in the recording and applied to half the
  position rather than resolving the failed scalp premise.
- Simulation fills and option prices cannot establish live executability,
  slippage, or profitability.
- The source repeatedly said it was comfortable with opposing orders, but did
  not state aggregate delta, combined maximum loss, or how one leg affected the
  other's exit.

## Falsifiable Replay Hypotheses

1. Test range scalps only when target distance exceeds spread plus fees by a
   specified multiple and compare them with trades taken near the middle of the
   range.
2. Test mandatory exit when a scalp's immediate target fails and price closes
   back through the entry structure versus allowing weekly options to recover.
3. Compare downside OMG close-bounce-entry with first-touch and first-close
   entries using executable option paths.
4. Measure the performance and attention cost of holding a losing opposing leg
   while taking new trades in the other direction.
5. Test Friday/weekend positions with predeclared full and partial invalidation
   rules against discretionary end-of-session decisions.
