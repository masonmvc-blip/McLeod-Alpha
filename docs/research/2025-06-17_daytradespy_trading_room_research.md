# McLeod Alpha Research Report: 2025-06-17 Trading Room

## Executive Assessment

June 17 contains a valuable distinction between a good outcome and a good
decision. A presenter disclosed that he had entered June 20 601 puts late and
heavily the prior afternoon at `4.20`, then sold them this morning at `4.80`.
He correctly called the trade a mistake despite the gain: late entry,
oversizing, and reliance on hoped-for reversal remained defects.

The room then entered upside calls before a formal OMG close. Real June 20 601
calls entered at `3.04` and 20 model calls at `3.05`; the speaker explicitly
said the OMG close had been missed “by a hair.” Both eventually worked, selling
at `3.35` and `3.39`. A later June 20 601-call trade waited for the prior
resistance to hold on a retest, entered `3.09`, and sold at `3.28`. That
accepted-break/retest sequence was the more reproducible entry even though all
three calls made money.

Two late scalps remained unresolved when the presenters left: June 20 600 calls
at `4.01` with a `4.15` target, and another presenter's June 20 601 calls with
an unstated entry and `3.44` target. The best lesson is to judge process
independently of outcome. Profitable rule-breaking should remain a process
failure, while a confirmed retest supplies testable evidence. Every trade also
needs resolution before the room closes or an explicit carry plan.

## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. Evaluate adherence separately from P&L; a profitable oversized late entry
   remains a process error.
2. A broken resistance level that holds as support is stronger admission
   evidence than anticipating the initial break.
3. If a rule can be waived through “interpretation,” the gate is not
   deterministic enough to test.
4. Avoid late re-entry after substantial expansion unless new structural room
   is demonstrated.
5. Close or explicitly classify every open scalp before ending the review.

## Contradictions and Process Risks

- The room acknowledged no OMG close but entered the early calls anyway.
- It then debated whether waiting for confirmation changed the rules.
- Profitable results may conceal the risk of late entry and oversizing.
- Two late scalps lacked final resolution.
- The second presenter's call lacked an audible entry premium.

## Falsifiable Replay Hypotheses

1. Compare pre-close anticipation with close-plus-retest entries.
2. Require the broken pivot to hold for one complete one-minute bar before
   admission.
3. Reject re-entry when remaining structural room is less than the planned
   target distance.
4. Force-close or formally convert every unresolved scalp at the review cutoff.
