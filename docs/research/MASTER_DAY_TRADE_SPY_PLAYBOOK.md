# Master Day Trade SPY Playbook

## Evidence Boundary

This playbook is a research synthesis, not a live-trading instruction set. The
2025 archive is now post-level complete: 230/230 posts have reports and
validated v2 records, representing 221 Tier C source reviews and nine Tier E
access/content/duplicate resolutions. It also considers 81 dated 2026 daily
reports through July 21. Most source reports lack broker fills, executable quote
paths, MFE/MAE, or chart reconstruction; recurrence broadens a hypothesis but
does not prove it. The July 21, 2026 report adds a reconciled McLeod Alpha
ledger fact set, but still lacks intratrade option excursions. See
`docs/research/2025_DAYTRADESPY_CROSS_YEAR_SYNTHESIS.md` for the completed
corpus audit and prioritized validation plan.

Evidence labels used below:

- **[Source]**: stated or shown in a Day Trade SPY recording.
- **[Ledger]**: independently reconciled McLeod Alpha completed-trade fact.
- **[Interpretation]**: synthesis of dated observations, not a demonstrated edge.
- **[Hypothesis]**: requires deterministic replay and out-of-sample validation.

## Ranked Principles

1. **Acceptance matters more than first contact.** [Source: 2025-02-10, 2025-02-26, 2026-05-14, 2026-06-08 to 12, 2026-07-16] A penetration, wick, or visual pattern is not enough. The recurring admissible sequence is close through a level, hold/retest, and follow-through. This is the strongest cross-session structural principle, but it is not yet expectancy evidence.
2. **Room to the next opposing structure is part of setup quality.** [Source: 2025-02-10, 2026-06-22 to 26, 2026-07-10, 2026-07-17, 2026-07-20, 2026-07-21] Pivots, prior extrema, VWAP, and multi-timeframe EMAs regularly compressed targets or invalidated continuation assumptions. [Interpretation] A signal whose intended path crosses nearby friction should be treated differently from one with open space.
3. **Opening follow-through and later continuation are different populations.** [Source: 2025-02-07, 2026-05-11, 2026-07-13, 2026-07-20, 2026-07-21] Fast opening moves can have immediate follow-through; later attempts often require a pullback, reclaim, or new breakout. [Ledger: 2026-07-21] A later call-only sequence was mixed after the initial opportunities. Do not generalize an opening scalp result to later re-entry.
4. **Trade structure, not direction, decides whether an EMA interaction is useful.** [Source: 2026-05-18, 2026-06-01 to 05, 2026-07-10, 2026-07-15] EMA touches are informative only with close-side acceptance, volume, multi-timeframe alignment, and remaining room. An isolated EMA bounce is not a setup.
5. **Congestion is a distinct no-trade regime.** [Source: 2026-05-19, 2026-06-08 to 12, 2026-07-16, 2026-07-21] Repeated pivot/EMA crossings, narrow range, failed closes, and overlapping averages indicate unresolved auction rather than repeated independent entries.
6. **A failed premise resets eligibility.** [Source: 2026-05-15, 2026-05-20, 2026-06-08 to 12, 2026-07-13, 2026-07-14] After a shock, target rejection, failed break, or loss of defining support, prior directional evidence is stale. Fresh support/reclaim/break acceptance is required.
7. **Options execution can invalidate an otherwise plausible underlying setup.** [Source: 2025-02-07, 2026-05-01, 2026-05-20, 2026-07-15] Midpoints and isolated last-trade highs do not establish executable outcomes. Bid, ask, mark, liquidity, delta, expiry, fill identity, and realized path are necessary.
8. **Do not defend a late or extended entry with optimism.** [Source: 2025-02-24, 2025-02-25, 2026-05-01, 2026-06-01 to 05, 2026-07-16] Extension into a structural zone, no pullback, fading volume, or repeated tests is a quality warning, not an invitation to increase conviction.

## Setup Taxonomy

### 1. Opening Follow-Through

**Context:** A directional opening displacement clears local congestion with relative-volume response and enough room before the next opposing structure. [Source: 2025-02-07, 2026-05-11, 2026-06-22 to 26, 2026-07-20]

**Admission sequence:**

1. Map opening-range boundary, pivot/prior extreme, VWAP/EMA cluster, and next opposing zone before the open.
2. Require a closed bar beyond the boundary, not a first touch. [Source: 2025-02-10, 2026-06-08 to 12]
3. Require volume displacement and either a post-break hold, retest hold, or new-extreme follow-through.
4. Confirm that the projected option objective has room before the nearest opposing friction.
5. Select an executable contract only after bid/ask, spread, volume/open interest, delta, expiry, and quote freshness pass.

**Invalidation/prohibitions:** First penetration without close/hold; low-volume break; broad two-sided opening range; immediate arrival at pivot/support/resistance; event shock without fresh acceptance; wide or stale option quote. [Source: 2026-05-19, 2026-05-27, 2026-06-01 to 05]

**Management:** Predefine a short-duration objective, then compress or bank it where the target path hits a pivot/R1/EMA cluster or acceptance weakens. [Source: 2026-07-10] Do not infer a fixed 6% target is achievable without executable option marks. [Source: 2025-02-07, 2025-02-10]

**Examples:** 2025-02-07 support/reversal sequence; 2026-05-14 break-retest-continuation; 2026-07-20 immediate opening call. **Negative examples:** 2026-05-19 narrow-range head fakes; 2026-06-08 to 12 first-break failures; 2026-07-21 later activity after opening structure.

### 2. Accepted Breakout

**Context:** Repeated or meaningful resistance/support zone is cleared after compression or consolidation; the relevant time frame confirms acceptance. [Source: 2025-02-27, 2026-05-11, 2026-07-16]

**Admission:** Define the zone, require close beyond it, then a hold/retest or fresh high/low with volume. Five-minute state frames the break; one-minute state supplies timing. [Source: 2025-02-27, 2026-06-22 to 26]

**Room/invalidation:** Require room from entry to next structural zone that supports the intended objective. Reject a break that returns inside the zone, cannot close beyond the next friction reference, or shows decaying volume. [Source: 2026-07-10, 2026-07-16]

**Management:** Structural objective first; reassess if price reaches friction but cannot accept. Do not raise a target merely because post-entry sentiment becomes bullish. [Source: 2025-02-27]

**Examples:** 2025-02-26 close below a recent-low zone; 2026-05-14 upside break/retest. **Negative examples:** 2026-05-01 low-volume break; 2026-06-22 to 26 first breakout reversal.

### 3. Failed Breakout and Reclaim

**Context:** A break/undercut fails, price reclaims the defining level, and a new direction develops. This is not simply “buy the dip” or “fade the break.” [Source: 2026-05-06, 2026-05-15, 2026-05-20, 2026-07-13]

**Admission:** Identify displacement and original break; require support hold or rejection, recovery volume, reclaim of local EMA/pivot, then clearance of intervening resistance. Reset prior continuation context. [Source: 2026-05-20, 2026-07-13]

**Room/invalidation:** Require room to the next opposing zone. Invalidate on loss of reclaimed support, failed post-reclaim hold, or reversal that remains below the larger intraday pivot/structure.

**Management:** Treat first structural objective as reassessment, not proof of durable trend. [Source: 2026-05-27] Preserve protective stops; source-side anti-stop opinions are not validated. [Source: 2026-07-17]

**Examples:** 2026-05-06 head-fake recovery; 2026-05-15 failed downside premise/reclaim; 2026-07-13 event-shock recovery. **Negative examples:** 2026-05-19 local bounce within continuing broader downtrend; 2026-05-27 recovery that may be retracement rather than reversal.

### 4. EMA/Support-Confluence Recovery

**Context:** Multiple independently measurable references coincide: pivot, prior extreme, retracement zone, VWAP/EMA, or range boundary. [Source: 2025-02-07, 2026-05-18, 2026-07-17]

**Admission:** Test the zone; require rejection/hold, improving or reversal volume, close/reclaim above the local trigger, and evidence of space to the next obstacle. A visible inverse-head-and-shoulders or divergence is not sufficient alone. [Source: 2026-05-18, 2026-05-20]

**Invalidation/prohibitions:** Close through defining support with adverse momentum; no reclaim; recovery into immediate five-minute resistance; weak option liquidity; merely hoping support holds. [Source: 2025-02-25, 2026-07-17]

**Management:** First target should respect nearby resistance. Record MFE, time at high, reversal depth, and subsequent reclaim/failure before evaluating any exit variant. [Source: 2026-07-14]

**Examples:** 2025-02-07 10/20 EMA support narrative; 2026-05-26 support/reclaim; 2026-07-10 20-EMA plus pre-market support recovery. **Negative examples:** 2025-02-25 support expectation without hard invalidation; 2026-05-18 unconfirmed visual reversal.

### 5. Trend Continuation After Pullback

**Context:** Existing trend has not exhausted, a pullback reaches a defined structural reference, and price re-accepts the trend direction. [Source: 2026-05-01, 2026-05-14, 2026-07-20]

**Admission:** Establish trend and structural room; require a real pullback or consolidation, support hold, reclaim/close in trend direction, renewed volume, and no nearby opposing barrier. Label trend age, leg count, normalized EMA distance, retracement depth, and volume phase.

**Invalidation/prohibitions:** Two-leg extended impulse without reset; zero/shallow pullback; price materially extended from short-horizon mean; volume fading into resistance; conflicting five-minute structure. [Source: 2026-05-01, 2026-05-14, 2026-07-15, 2026-07-20]

**Management:** Use structural objective/room, not unconditional extension. A target that runs through friction is compressed or not admitted. [Source: 2026-07-10, 2026-07-17]

**Examples:** 2026-05-14 post-break pullback and new highs; 2026-07-20 opening versus later reclaim contrast. **Negative examples:** 2025-02-24 late put after extension; 2026-07-16 rapid EMA push into resistance.

### 6. Congestion / No-Trade Regime

**Context:** Overlapping EMAs, narrow realized range, repeated pivot/VWAP/EMA crossings, failed breaks, and oscillation around the same zone. [Source: 2026-05-19, 2026-06-08 to 12, 2026-07-16, 2026-07-21]

**Admission:** None until price closes and holds beyond the congestion boundary with volume and structural room. The first breakout is only a candidate; the accepted breakout rules apply.

**Prohibit:** Re-entry because direction “looks due,” repeated same-regime trades, targets through range boundaries, and interpretation of isolated EMA crosses as trend. [Source: 2026-07-21]

**Management:** If already positioned, objective expectations should be limited by range edges; re-entry requires a new accepted break. [Source: 2026-07-10, 2026-07-21]

**Examples:** 2026-05-19 opening head fakes; 2026-07-16 pivot/S1/50-EMA “dance”; 2026-07-21 range-bound later tape. **Negative evidence:** no report supplies executable performance proving a congestion filter improves expectancy.

### 7. Event-Shock Recovery

**Context:** Gap, scheduled release, or headline displacement produces abnormally large/fast range and often reverses ordinary technical behavior. [Source: 2026-05-06, 2026-05-15, 2026-05-20, 2026-06-01 to 05, 2026-07-13]

**Admission:** Flag event window and shock magnitude; suspend inherited continuation premise; require support/rejection, pivot reclaim, sequential EMA acceptance, volume response, and room before a recovery is eligible.

**Prohibit:** Immediate fade/reversal based solely on oversold/overbought, ordinary opening-break rules during unresolved shock, and treating macro narration as a directional predictor. [Source: 2026-05-06, 2026-05-19]

**Management:** Reduce reliance on static targets; evaluate premise failure/reclaim with executable marks, abnormal spreads, IV/theta, and time to resolution. [Source: 2026-05-15, 2026-05-20]

**Examples:** 2026-05-06 ceasefire/energy displacement; 2026-05-15 gap lower/recovery; 2026-07-13 rapid adverse impulse/recovery. **Negative examples:** 2026-05-19 scheduled-release whipsaw; 2026-05-27 headline spike retraced before open.

## Unified Decision Process

1. **Premarket map:** Record prior high/low, premarket high/low, opening-range candidates, pivots, VWAP, one- and five-minute EMA clusters, gaps, scheduled-event windows, and data-health flags. Treat levels as zones, not exact promises. [Source: 2025-02-26, 2026-05-26]
2. **Classify session state:** Opening follow-through, accepted breakout, reclaim, continuation-after-pullback, congestion, or event shock. Do not force a label; `NO_ADMISSION` is valid.
3. **Construct premise:** Direction, defining level, structural room, invalidation, and target region must be known before entry.
4. **Require sequence:** Close-side confirmation, acceptance/hold/retest, volume response, time-frame alignment, and sufficient room. An indicator or pattern alone cannot pass.
5. **Check option executability:** Fresh bid/ask/mark; bounded spread; adequate volume/open interest; appropriate delta/expiry; observable fill identity. Reject unavailable or stale execution data.
6. **Manage:** Preserve protective stop; watch defining support/resistance, acceptance failure, option bid/mark, MFE/MAE, and time-at-peak. Compress objective at intervening friction. A premise failure requires reassessment, not averaging/reloading.
7. **Exit and stop for the day:** Stop new entries after unresolved congestion, repeated failed same-regime attempts, event-data unreliability, or risk controls. [Source: 2026-07-21] Do not infer a universal trade count from source commentary; test any count friction separately.

## When Not To Trade

- First touch/penetration without close, hold, or volume: 2025-02-10; 2026-06-08 to 12.
- Extension into immediate support/resistance or after multiple impulse legs without reset: 2025-02-24; 2026-05-01; 2026-07-16.
- Pullback is absent, shallow, or lacks reclaim/hold: 2026-07-20; 2026-07-14.
- Repeated crossings, overlapping EMAs, narrow range, failed breaks: 2026-05-19; 2026-07-16; 2026-07-21.
- Recovery is only a visual pattern or optimism, not structural acceptance: 2025-02-25; 2026-05-18.
- Event shock remains unresolved or scheduled-release reaction is two-sided: 2026-05-06; 2026-05-19; 2026-07-13.
- The option contract has wide/stale bid-ask, low liquidity, unreliable display/order state, or unknown execution path: 2025-02-07; 2026-05-01; 2026-05-20; 2026-07-15.
- A previous target rejection/stop occurred in the same unresolved structure: 2026-07-14; 2026-07-21.

## Mistakes and General Lessons

| Corpus example | General lesson |
|---|---|
| Late put acknowledged after an extended decline (2025-02-24) | Separate trend direction from entry location; late momentum is not the same setup as early accepted breakdown. |
| Holding calls on expectation of support without hard invalidation (2025-02-25) | A support thesis is not risk control. Define premise failure before entry. |
| Raising a fixed target because of bullish feeling (2025-02-27) | Target revision changes the strategy distribution; record it as a separate treatment, not as success of the original plan. |
| Low-volume breakout / head-fake conditions (2026-05-01, 2026-05-06) | Break quality requires acceptance and volume, not chart appearance. |
| Treating a sharp undercut as reversal before recovery sequence (2026-05-20) | New low alone is not a reversal signal; require reclaim plus follow-through. |
| Seven later mixed calls in range conditions after early opportunities [Ledger: 2026-07-21] | Re-entry quality must be conditioned on unresolved regime, not only directional score. |
| Isolated option high could be one-lot/non-executable (2026-07-15) | Last price/high cannot prove target feasibility or exit quality. |

## Unsupported or Contradictory Claims

**Frequently repeated but unproven:** fixed 6% option targets; percentage/fixed-dollar target superiority; particular expiry choice for fast puts; EMA/Fibonacci/pitchfork labels as standalone edge; high-level macro narrative as timing signal; and source-side call/put examples as directional bias. These repeat across reports but lack synchronized bid/ask, fills, and controlled comparison.

**Discipline versus observed management tension:**

- Source statements favor predefined targets, yet targets may be raised after entry or compressed ad hoc near friction. [Source: 2025-02-27; 2026-07-10]
- Source cautions against chasing, yet describes late entries and support-based holding without a quantified invalidation. [Source: 2025-02-24, 2025-02-25]
- Same-day technical framing is mixed with overnight fallback in the January 21, 2025 report; these have materially different risk distributions. [Source: 2025-01-21]
- Source commentary occasionally questions stops, while the corpus supplies no executable adverse-excursion evidence that would justify weakening a tested protective-stop policy. [Source: 2026-07-17]

## Ranked Falsifiable Replay Agenda

### 1. Accepted Breakout vs First Penetration

- **Hypothesis:** close-plus-hold/retest breaks have better executable excursion than first penetration.
- **Labels:** opening range/pivot zone; close-through distance; retest/hold; follow-through high/low; relative volume.
- **Data:** one- and five-minute SPY bars, volume, pivot/EMA/VWAP reconstruction, option bid/ask/mark, fills, costs, MFE/MAE.
- **Baseline/treatment:** current direction-score entry versus accepted-break gate.
- **Negative controls:** randomly delayed entry; same-direction first penetrations with no hold; shuffled level labels.
- **Segmentation:** opening/later session, event window, gap size, trend/congestion state, direction.
- **Metrics:** net expectancy after costs, profit factor, target hit, MFE/MAE, drawdown, trade count, exposure.
- **Falsify:** no out-of-sample improvement or material drawdown/sample-collapse penalty.
- **Validation:** chronological train/validation/test blocks and rolling walk-forward; do not tune on July alone.
- **Priority:** 1. Evidence: 2025-02-10; 2026-06-08 to 12; 2026-07-16.

### 2. Structural Room-to-Target Gate

- **Hypothesis:** sufficient distance to next opposing pivot/VWAP/EMA/range boundary improves target achievement and risk-adjusted expectancy.
- **Labels:** direction-specific nearest friction, room in underlying ATR and projected option-return units, intervening structure count.
- **Data:** reconstructed structures plus executable option path and costs.
- **Baseline/treatment:** baseline versus minimum-room gate and structural target cap.
- **Negative controls:** equal numerical threshold to irrelevant same-side level; randomized friction ordering.
- **Segmentation:** setup class, time of day, trend age, event window, option spread bucket.
- **Metrics/falsification/validation:** as experiment 1, plus missed-opportunity cost and target compression accuracy; reject absent OOS gain.
- **Priority:** 2. Evidence: 2026-07-10, 2026-07-17, 2026-07-20, 2026-07-21.

### 3. Congestion No-Admission and Re-entry Friction

- **Hypothesis:** blocking/raising threshold after repeated crossings and failed breaks reduces loss/drawdown more than it sacrifices positive expectancy.
- **Labels:** realized range, EMA compression, pivot/VWAP crossings, failed close attempts, same-regime entry count.
- **Data:** minute bars, decision logs including rejected signals, option telemetry, trade ledger.
- **Baseline/treatment:** baseline versus congestion gate; separate after-two same-regime attempts variant.
- **Negative controls:** equal trade-count penalty outside congestion; randomized crossing counts.
- **Segmentation:** opening/later, high/low volatility, event/non-event, bullish/bearish regime.
- **Metrics/falsification/validation:** net after costs, PF, drawdown, turnover, rejected-trade counterfactual; reject if OOS improvement comes only from trade removal or raises drawdown.
- **Priority:** 3. Evidence: 2026-05-19, 2026-07-16, 2026-07-21.

### 4. Pullback Quality and Extension Filter

- **Hypothesis:** continuation after defined retracement plus reclaim outperforms zero/shallow-pullback or extended entries.
- **Labels:** trend age/legs, distance from EMA10 in ATR/percent, retracement depth/time, reclaim close, volume renewal.
- **Data:** minute bars, multi-timeframe features, option paths/costs.
- **Baseline/treatment:** baseline continuation versus require-pullback-or-reclaim; test extension penalty separately.
- **Negative controls:** same threshold applied before opening follow-through; randomized pullback labels.
- **Segmentation:** opening follow-through versus later continuation, gap/event, EMA spread bucket.
- **Metrics/falsification/validation:** as experiment 1; reject if only a single high-extension week drives result.
- **Priority:** 4. Evidence: 2025-02-24, 2026-05-01, 2026-07-15, 2026-07-20.

### 5. Premise-Failure / Event-Shock Reset

- **Hypothesis:** after abnormal shock or defining-level failure, requiring fresh reclaim/acceptance improves outcomes versus carrying forward the prior signal.
- **Labels:** normalized shock range/volume/reversal speed, event window, support hold, pivot reclaim, EMA sequence, resistance clearance.
- **Data:** events calendar, SPY bars/volume, option quotes, fills, MFE/MAE.
- **Baseline/treatment:** normal continuation logic versus cooldown/fresh-reclaim requirement.
- **Negative controls:** equal cooldown in quiet trend minutes; shock label with scrambled event times.
- **Segmentation:** scheduled versus headline proxy, gap size, opening/later, volatility regime.
- **Metrics/falsification/validation:** after-cost expectancy, MAE, time-to-resolution, drawdown, skipped-winner rate; reject no OOS reduction in adverse excursion or expectancy improvement.
- **Priority:** 5. Evidence: 2026-05-06, 2026-05-15, 2026-05-20, 2026-07-13.

## Final Conclusions

### A. Best Lesson

**A SPY setup is not a direction or indicator; it is a sequence of acceptance, room, and executable pricing.** The corpus most consistently warns that first moves, pretty patterns, and targets without an executable path are not enough. [Source: 2026-06-01 to 05; 2026-07-15]

### B. Five Rules Most Ready for Testing

1. Accepted breakout requires close plus hold/retest.
2. Minimum structural room before entry/target.
3. Congestion no-admission/re-entry friction.
4. Pullback-or-reclaim requirement for later continuation.
5. Event-shock reset requiring fresh structure.

### C. Five Most Dangerous Unsupported Assumptions

1. A stated 6% option target was executable or filled.
2. Option last/high represents a tradeable exit.
3. Repetition of an EMA/Fibonacci/pattern idea establishes edge.
4. A source-side macro narrative supplies intraday direction.
5. A visual support level justifies holding, averaging, or reloading without quantified invalidation.

### D. Highest-Value Data to Collect

Per management cycle: timestamped option bid, ask, mark, last, quote age/source, spread, option high/low, MFE/MAE, current stop, decision action/reason, and broker order/fill IDs. At entry/exit: actual fill, commissions/fees, delta, IV, expiry, volume/open interest, and slippage. For replay: synchronized one- and five-minute SPY bars, volume, reconstructed structural zones, event-window tags, all evaluated/rejected signals, and canonical ledger linkage. Without these, the corpus can rank hypotheses but cannot establish profitability or expectancy.
