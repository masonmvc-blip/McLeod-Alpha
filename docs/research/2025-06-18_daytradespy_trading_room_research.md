# McLeod Alpha Research Report: 2025-06-18 Trading Room

## Executive Assessment

June 18 is unusually useful because the profitable opening sequence and the
late-session behavior point in opposite directions. The opening downside break
landed directly at support and reversed. Calls entered after that failed break
and recovery produced several source-reported gains. The most disciplined
late trade was a high-risk same-day 601-call scalp: entry `1.29`, a bid-based
trailing stop, and exit `1.49`.

The process deteriorated after the room had already exceeded its daily profit
goal. A presenter acknowledged greed and carelessness, then said he could not
let the move continue without being involved. Further calls were entered, one
remained open, and an OMG put was held against the accepted uptrend through the
Federal Reserve event. This makes the session a strong test case for a
profit-lock rule: once the daily objective is reached, only a pre-defined
A-grade setup with fixed risk should permit re-entry.

The central lesson is not simply to buy a failed downside break. It is that
confirmation and bounded risk produced the cleanest trades, while event hope,
fear of missing the continuation, and repeated re-entry created unresolved
exposure.

## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. When a breakout reaches support before entry and immediately reclaims the
   boundary, the breakout has insufficient structural room.
2. Failed-break recovery entries were stronger than holding the original
   directional thesis.
3. A high-risk 0DTE trade is only research-grade when entry, stop mechanism,
   and exit are all explicit.
4. Re-entry after the daily goal is reached needs a higher admission threshold,
   not a lower one.
5. “I cannot miss this move” is a disqualifying motive unless an independently
   specified setup and risk budget are present.
6. Event exposure does not replace price invalidation.

## Contradictions and Process Risks

- The downside OMG put was retained after the opening break failed and price
  accepted higher.
- The speaker recognized greed before taking another trade.
- The room continued trading after exceeding its stated daily objective.
- The 599-call entry, exit, and stated gain conflict within the source.
- The final 600 calls and OMG puts lacked audible resolution.
- Targets changed after entry without a stated structural reason.

## Falsifiable Replay Hypotheses

1. After reaching the daily profit objective, compare stopping for the day with
   permitting only one pre-labelled A-grade re-entry.
2. Reject breakouts whose entry occurs after price has already reached the next
   support/resistance level.
3. Exit a directional position when price closes back through the broken
   boundary and accepts in the opposite direction.
4. Require every event-held option to have an explicit price invalidation and
   maximum-loss amount before the event begins.
