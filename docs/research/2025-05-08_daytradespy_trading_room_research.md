# McLeod Alpha Research Report: 2025-05-08 Trading Room

## Executive Assessment

The complete authorized caption track changes this recording from an evidence
gap into a useful process study. The source reported a fast, rule-shaped
downside OMG trade, but simultaneously carried an early bullish call position
entered while price remained below falling moving averages and a scheduled
trade-policy announcement was still pending. The OMG put reportedly reached
its fixed premium target in about five minutes. The calls moved materially
against the source before a later event-driven recovery produced a reported
target fill.

The highest-value lesson is not that both directions eventually paid. It is that
the two trades had very different decision quality. The put had a close below a
defined level, a bounce/rollover entry sequence, an underlying-linked target,
and a prompt exit. The early call relied on a forecast that support and favorable
news would eventually rescue the position; the source stated no stop or
invalidation and held through adverse movement. A profitable recovery does not
validate that management process.

## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. **Prefer a close, retest, and renewed move over first contact.** The downside
   OMG sequence required a close below the boundary, a bounce, and a rollover.
   That sequence is much more testable than entering at the first breach.
2. **Structural room belongs in admission.** The presenters rejected the closer
   upside boundary because nearby right-shoulder resistance left too little
   room. This is directly consistent with the master playbook.
3. **A target does not replace an invalidation.** The early call had a target
   but no source-stated stop while price remained below falling averages and
   continued lower. Event optimism became the holding rationale.
4. **A later profitable recovery can conceal weak risk control.** The call's
   reported `6.66` to `7.00` result should not be learned as permission to hold
   indefinitely. Without MAE, time-underwater, or a maximum-loss rule, the
   process cannot be judged safe.
5. **Opposing positions require portfolio-level risk accounting.** Simultaneous
   calls and puts may express different time horizons, but the recording gave
   no combined delta, maximum loss, or rule for which thesis controlled.
6. **The later call had better evidence.** A failed 50-MA breakout, subsequent
   hold of the 10/20 MAs, descending-trend-line break, and retest preceded the
   564-call trade. That sequence is a stronger replay candidate than the early
   forecast-driven call.

## Contradictions and Process Risks

- The source emphasized patience and waiting for support confirmation, then
  entered calls while all referenced moving averages remained overhead.
- The source recognized open downside room and weak bounce volume but continued
  to characterize the call decline as temporary without stating an invalidation.
- “Playing the statistics” was used to explain the successful OMG, but no
  sample, regime segmentation, transaction costs, or failure distribution was
  provided.
- The eventual event-driven recovery is outcome evidence, not proof that the
  original bullish entry or hold was well controlled.
- Caption-only speaker attribution is imperfect; the call positions must not be
  silently merged into one trade.

## Falsifiable Replay Hypotheses

1. Test downside OMG entries requiring boundary close, bounce, and rollover
   against first-touch and first-close baselines, net of spread and fees.
2. Test whether rejecting triggers with less than a specified room-to-resistance
   threshold improves expectancy and reduces premature reversals.
3. Compare bullish support-reclaim entries taken below falling one- and
   five-minute moving averages with entries delayed until trend-line break,
   MA recovery, and successful retest.
4. Measure whether scheduled-event entries made before the event have worse
   MAE and tail loss than post-event acceptance entries, even when both later
   reach the same target.
5. Evaluate simultaneous opposing option legs only with aggregate delta,
   combined realized P&L, maximum adverse excursion, and leg-specific
   invalidations.
