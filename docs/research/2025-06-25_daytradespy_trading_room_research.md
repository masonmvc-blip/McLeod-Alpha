# McLeod Alpha Research Report: 2025-06-25 Trading Room

## Executive Assessment

June 25 began with an explicit wait for support, but the room then accumulated
four overlapping July 3 call positions plus the published pick while price
remained choppy and two major speakers were active. A qualifying upside OMG
close did occur, and the room entered July 3 608 calls at `5.40` with a `5.72`
target. Price subsequently reversed to a new session low. The OMG calls, a
two-80 position, a real 609-call scalp, an added 608-call position, and the
published 610-call pick were all still unresolved at the end of the recording.

The useful lesson is not that the qualifying close was invalid. It is that
signal validity and trade risk are separate questions. A valid close can still
fail, especially when entry is followed by clustered exposure, no audible
price invalidation, and simultaneous Powell and Trump headline risk.

## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. Preserve the close requirement, but pair it with an independent maximum-loss
   rule; a qualifying signal is not a guarantee.
2. Cap correlated exposure across a named setup, discretionary scalp, model
   trade, and published pick.
3. Do not add a recovery position while prior positions lack a defined
   invalidation and aggregate risk is unknown.
4. Treat simultaneous scheduled and unscheduled speakers as an explicit event
   state, not ordinary intraday noise.
5. Record unresolved positions as unresolved; confidence in a later bounce is
   not an exit.

## Contradictions and Process Risks

- The room initially waited for support, but accumulated calls before and after

- The qualifying upside close was followed by a new session low.
- A later ABCD recovery was described first as failed and then as a possible
  setup again, without a fixed invalidation.
- Targets remained in place while the market moved adversely.
- Five call positions were unresolved at recording end.

## Falsifiable Replay Hypotheses

1. Compare qualifying-close entries with and without a fixed structural stop.
2. Test an aggregate correlated-exposure cap across simultaneous SPY calls.
3. Prohibit adding recovery positions until the original trade is flat or its
   remaining risk is mechanically bounded.
4. Compare scheduled-speaker windows with otherwise similar OMG signals.
