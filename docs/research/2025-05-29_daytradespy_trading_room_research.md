# McLeod Alpha Research Report: 2025-05-29 Trading Room

## Executive Assessment

The cleanest trade was the downside OMG. After a confirmed lower close, June 6
591 puts entered at `5.64` with room toward lower support and reached a `5.98`
target within about five minutes; the source observed a `5.99` bid. This is a
strong example of confirmation, structural room, and a bounded objective
working together even though the presenters still anticipated a possible
bounce.

The dominant process failure was the May 28 call carried overnight. The May 30
592 calls entered at `4.35` opened near `3.31-3.34`, had no target, consumed
attention throughout the recording, and remained unresolved. The presenter
explicitly identified the original re-entry as aggressive and as greed after a
good first-day return. May 30 later confirms that the position was sold for a
loss after this recording.

Two late call sequences were directionally better because they waited for an
upside recovery, but their records are incomplete. One quick 591-call trade was
reported as “taken out” without premiums. A June 6 590-call trade reached an
initial 50-EMA objective and was reportedly taken out near `6.75`, but its entry
premium was not stated; an immediate second entry remained unresolved at the
bell.

The best lesson is that a losing carry should not be allowed to consume the
decision process. Each new trade must be judged independently, and unresolved
legacy exposure must have a hard exit plan before another position is admitted.

## Entry and Exit Lessons

1. The downside OMG worked because it required a confirmed boundary close and
   retained room before the next meaningful support.
2. An overnight loser without a maximum loss becomes an attention tax and
   encourages breakeven anchoring.
3. A prior loss should not change the admission standard for a new setup.
4. Late recovery calls were more defensible only after price reclaimed the
   short-term averages and resistance.
5. Immediate re-entry after an exit creates a new trade and requires a complete
   new plan, even if the directional thesis is unchanged.

## Contradictions and Process Risks

- The carried call was initially described with the wrong strike/entry before
  being corrected.
- The presenter recognized greed and aggressive admission but still provided no
  hard exit for the position.
- The room's cleanest trade was a put while discussion continued to favor an
  eventual bounce; evidence outweighed opinion.
- Two late calls lacked entry premiums, making their stated success
  unauditable.
- A new late call was added while the prior-day losing call remained unresolved.

## Falsifiable Replay Hypotheses

1. Force-close any carried option at a predefined loss boundary before admitting
   a new intraday trade.
2. Compare confirmed downside-OMG entries with trades based only on the
   presenter's directional expectation.
3. Measure whether legacy losing exposure reduces adherence or expectancy on
   otherwise valid new setups.
4. Require a fresh setup record and invalidation before every same-direction
   re-entry.
5. Reject any result from performance statistics unless both entry and exit
   premiums are captured.
